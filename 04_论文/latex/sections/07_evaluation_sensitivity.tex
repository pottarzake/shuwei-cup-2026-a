\section{Comprehensive Evaluation and Global Sensitivity}

\subsection{Evaluation Indicator System}
To rank stimulation strategies beyond a single objective, we define seven
indicators, each with a clear unit and direction (Table~\ref{tbl:indicators}).
Indicators are oriented so that higher normalized scores are preferred.
Table~\ref{tbl:indicators} compares the scenarios at their economic optima.
Case~3 has the strongest productivity and absolute NPV under the reference
operation. The stimulation radius $R(t_s^*)$
(from Section~4) and the robustness margin (from Section~5) plug into the
same framework as additional axes.

\subsection{Nested Sobol Sensitivity Analysis}
Following \cite{p4-02}, we compute variance-based Sobol indices with
Saltelli sampling ($N = 1024$, second-order terms included). The sampling
uses three complementary matrix families: $A_B^{(i)}$ (column $i$ from $A$)
for first-order indices, $A_B^{(\sim i)}$ (column $i$ from $B$) for
total-effect indices, and $A_B^{(ij)}$ for second-order terms; all outputs
are mean-centered before forming moments to avoid the catastrophic
cancellation $f_0^2 \gg V_i$ that otherwise corrupts first-order estimates
when outputs are large. The forward model is the T22-calibrated
reduced-order evaluator (\S5) with volume/fraction scaling exponents
calibrated to essentially zero (T31), so injected volume and fraction act
through costs only; eleven inputs are sampled uniformly ($k_{m0}$,
$\phi_0$, $\sigma_h$, $\sigma_H$, $E$, $C_n$, $V_{inj}$, $q_{inj}$,
$f_{CO2}$, $t_s$, $\rho_{nf}$). For $Q_{360}$, $t_s$ enters as an input; for the
economic optimum $t_s^*$ it is excluded and each outer sample scans
$t_s \in \{5, 10, \dots, 60\}$~d over the feasible set, taking the
NPV-maximizing level (ties to the shorter soak); outer samples with no
feasible soak are counted and reported (they enter as the shortest level).

For $Q_{360}$ the ranking is consistent across scenarios
(Figure~\ref{fig:sobol}): soak time and porosity dominate
(total-effect indices in Case~3: $ST_{t_s} = 0.63$, $ST_{\phi_0} = 0.31$),
while injected volume, CO$_2$ fraction, matrix permeability and the
stress/elastic parameters contribute essentially nothing to the
production response---consistent with the T31 finding that the calibrated
volume/fraction exponents are zero; second-order terms are small
($\leq 0.01$ for the top pair), i.e.\ the production response is nearly
additive in the two leading parameters. For $t_s^*$ the picture is
scenario-dependent. Case~1 has
an empty feasible region for every outer sample (100\%, consistent with
the P3 optimization), so no $t_s^*$ distribution exists---soak-time
optimization is moot where the design is infeasible. Case~2 is dominated by
the injection rate ($ST_{q_{inj}} = 0.94$) and matrix permeability
($ST_{k_{m0}} = 0.46$, largely through the $k_{m0} \times q_{inj}$
interaction $S_{ij} = 0.30$): with the feasibility boundary cutting
through the sampled box, $t_s^*$ is controlled by whether the fracture
pressure constraint leaves any room for soaking at all. Case~3, where
feasibility is rarely binding (8.3\% infeasible), $t_s^*$ is controlled by
the economic drivers---injected volume, CO$_2$ fraction and nano-
concentration ($ST = 0.65$, $0.62$, $0.62$)---exactly the direction of the
reference ranking of \cite{p4-04} (injected volume first), corroborating
the model. The Case~2 deviation (rate- and permeability-bound) is
explained by our additional nano and CO$_2$ decision variables, the
single-cycle design, and the explicit pressure-feasibility constraint,
none of which enter the reference study's ranking. Sensitivity of NPV at
the optimum shows the same pattern and is reported in the supplementary
tables. One limitation is stated openly: $E$, $\sigma_H$ and $\rho_{nf}$
are inert under the current reduced-order forward model (their indices are
$\approx 0$ by construction rather than by physics); this is resolved when
the analysis is re-run on the mechanistic forward model of Section~4,
which uses the identical sampling and estimator code, developed with AI assistance \cite{deepseek,codex}.
